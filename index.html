About

Mathematics with a probabilistic lens.

My work centers on probability theory, distributional approximation, and Stein's method, with an emphasis on methods that are mathematically rigorous and useful in applications.

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Mathematics and Computer Science, Faculty of Science, Chulalongkorn University.

I received my Ph.D. in Mathematics from Oregon State University in 2021, under the supervision of Professor Yevgeniy Kovchegov.

My research interests lie in probability theory, distributional approximation, and Stein's method. I am particularly interested in developing probabilistic approximation techniques and applying them to both theoretical and practical problems.

Research

Current directions.

I develop approximation techniques for stochastic models, with a particular focus on explicit error bounds and methods inspired by Stein's method.

01 · ZERO-BIAS

Approximate Zero-Bias Transformations

Development of approximate zero-bias techniques for normal approximation, including extensions to random sums and non-identically distributed summands.

02 · RANDOM SUMS

Random Sums & Applications

Normal approximation for random sums arising in applied stochastic models, including sampling with outliers, insurance, and generative AI response times.

03 · FUNCTIONAL STEIN

Functional Stein's Method and Random Fields

I am interested in functional versions of Stein's method and their applications to random fields, including Gaussian free fields.

04 · POISSON

Poisson Approximation

Poisson approximation with applications to stop-loss metrics and other probability models where explicit approximation guarantees are useful.
